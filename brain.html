HOW TO USE THIS PAGE

When the user enters an examination keyword (for example “pituitary”, “IAC”, “brain normal”), locate the matching section below and return the FINDINGS and CONCLUSION text for that section verbatim, unless the user supplies abnormal findings. If the user supplies abnormal findings, keep the template structure and the untouched normal statements, and replace only the relevant sentences.

Do not invent measurements, laterality or clinical history. If a value is required and has not been supplied, insert a placeholder in square brackets, for example [X] mm.

All macro output is a draft only. The reporting radiologist must verify every statement against the images before the report is issued.

REPORTING RULES (apply to every macro on this page)

RULE 1 — When an examination keyword is entered alone, return the matching normal template below in full. Do not ask clarifying questions first; return the draft, then invite corrections.

RULE 2 — When abnormal findings are supplied, keep the template structure and retain every normal statement that has not been contradicted. Replace only the affected sentences and update the conclusion.

RULE 3 — Never invent measurements, laterality, spinal levels, PSA values, dates, clinical history or comparison studies. Insert a square-bracket placeholder such as [X] mm or [left/right] instead.

RULE 4 — Report order: CLINICAL, TECHNIQUE, COMPARISON, FINDINGS, CONCLUSION. Omit sections not supplied. Findings are continuous prose, not bullet points.

RULE 5 — UK English spelling throughout: haemorrhage, oedema, visualised, tumour, calibre.

RULE 6 — The conclusion answers the clinical question and does not repeat the findings.

RULE 7 — Every draft is a draft only. The reporting radiologist must verify each statement against the images before the report is issued.

RULE 8 — If no macro exists for the examination requested, say so and offer a generic structured template rather than improvising from memory.

BRAIN

Trigger: brain
Keywords: brain, brain normal, normal brain, MRI head, MRI brain, routine brain

FINDINGS

The ventricles and sulci are normal in size and configuration for the patient’s age. Normal grey-white matter differentiation. No focal parenchymal signal abnormality on T2 or FLAIR imaging. No restricted diffusion to indicate acute infarction. No intracranial haemorrhage or susceptibility abnormality. No mass lesion, midline shift or mass effect. No extra-axial collection. No abnormal enhancement following contrast. The major intracranial arterial flow voids are preserved. The brainstem, cerebellum and craniocervical junction are normal. The visualised orbits, paranasal sinuses and mastoid air cells are clear.

CONCLUSION

Normal MRI of the brain.

Key negatives: no acute infarct; no haemorrhage; no mass lesion; no hydrocephalus; no extra-axial collection; no abnormal enhancement.

PITUITARY

Trigger: pituitary
Keywords: pituitary, pituitary MRI, normal pituitary, sella, sellar, microadenoma, hypophysis

FINDINGS

The pituitary gland is normal in height, morphology and signal. The posterior pituitary bright spot is present and normally sited. No focal lesion is identified on dynamic post-contrast imaging, with symmetrical uniform enhancement of the gland. The infundibulum is midline and of normal calibre, with no thickening. The sellar floor is intact. No suprasellar extension and no mass effect upon the optic chiasm. The cavernous sinuses are symmetrical with no invasion, and the cavernous internal carotid arteries are normal. The sphenoid sinus is clear. The visualised brain parenchyma is unremarkable.

CONCLUSION

Normal appearances of the pituitary gland. No evidence of pituitary microadenoma or other sellar or parasellar lesion.

Key negatives: no focal pituitary lesion; stalk midline; no suprasellar extension; no optic chiasm compression; no cavernous sinus invasion.

Reporting notes: assess gland height against age and sex norms, stalk for deviation or thickening, the chiasm for compression, and the cavernous sinuses for invasion. Comment on the posterior pituitary bright spot where diabetes insipidus is the indication.

IAC

Trigger: IAC
Keywords: IAC, IAM, internal auditory canal, internal auditory meatus, acoustic, vestibular schwannoma, CPA, cerebellopontine angle, hearing loss, tinnitus

FINDINGS

The internal auditory canals are symmetrical and of normal calibre. Four discrete nerves are identified within each internal auditory canal on high-resolution heavily T2-weighted imaging, with normal appearances of the seventh and eighth cranial nerve complexes bilaterally. No vestibular schwannoma or other cerebellopontine angle mass. No abnormal enhancement following contrast. The cochlea, vestibule and semicircular canals demonstrate normal fluid signal bilaterally. The middle ear cavities and mastoid air cells are clear. The brainstem and cerebellum are normal, with no focal parenchymal lesion to account for the symptoms.

CONCLUSION

Normal MRI of the internal auditory canals. No vestibular schwannoma or other cerebellopontine angle mass.

Key negatives: no vestibular schwannoma; no CPA mass; normal VII and VIII nerve complexes; no abnormal enhancement; clear mastoid air cells.

ORBITS

Trigger: orbits
Keywords: orbits, orbit, optic nerve, optic neuritis, proptosis, thyroid eye disease, visual loss

FINDINGS

The globes are normal in size, contour and signal. The optic nerves are of normal calibre and signal throughout their intraorbital, intracanalicular and intracranial segments, with no abnormal enhancement. The optic nerve sheath complexes are unremarkable. The optic chiasm and optic tracts are normal. The extraocular muscles are normal in bulk and signal, including their tendinous insertions. The orbital fat is normal with no infiltration, and no intraconal or extraconal mass is identified. The lacrimal glands are symmetrical and normal. No proptosis. The cavernous sinuses, superior ophthalmic veins and paranasal sinuses are unremarkable. The visualised brain parenchyma is normal.

CONCLUSION

Normal MRI of the orbits. No evidence of optic neuritis or orbital mass.

Key negatives: normal optic nerve signal and calibre; no abnormal enhancement; normal extraocular muscles; no orbital mass; no proptosis.

MS

Trigger: MS
Keywords: MS, multiple sclerosis, demyelination, demyelinating, white matter, McDonald criteria

FINDINGS

No T2 or FLAIR hyperintense lesions are identified in the periventricular, juxtacortical or cortical, infratentorial or brainstem locations. The callososeptal interface and corpus callosum are normal. No lesion demonstrates restricted diffusion. No enhancing lesion following contrast. No cerebral volume loss beyond that expected for the patient’s age. The visualised upper cervical cord is normal in signal and calibre.

CONCLUSION

Normal MRI of the brain. No lesions to suggest demyelination, and no lesions fulfilling criteria for dissemination in space.

Key negatives: no periventricular, juxtacortical, infratentorial or cord lesions; no enhancing lesion; normal corpus callosum.

Reporting notes: where lesions are present, record the number and location by McDonald anatomical region, state whether any lesion enhances, and comment explicitly on dissemination in space and in time.

FINDINGS

Time-of-flight angiographic images of the intracranial circulation. The intracranial internal carotid arteries are patent and of normal calibre bilaterally. The anterior cerebral arteries, middle cerebral arteries and their major branches are normal, with no stenosis or occlusion.
